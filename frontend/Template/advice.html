Why choose a T Level

Learn why you should consider a T level. Knowing about them early helps you plan for you future.

  • You learn by doing

    Lessons are practical and built around a careers, not only exams.

  • You get a real placement

    Spend at least 315 hours with an employer. You learn what the job is really like and build contacts.

  • It counts like three A Levels

    A T Level is worth the same as three A Levels, so it is a serious qualification.

  • It keeps doors open

    After a T Level you can go to university, start an apprenticeship or go straight into work.

In Year 10 now?

You can explore the five routes, talk to your careers advisor, and register your interest so we can keep you updated. Nothing is locked in.

How to apply for a placement

Placements are for students on a T Level course. These are the steps, in order.

  1. Pick a T Level

    Choose a T Level course at a school or college near you. Ask your careers advisor which courses they offer.

  2. Register your interest

    Tell us which routes you like. We'll email you when applications open. You can change your answers later.

  3. Talk to your school or college

    They organise placements for their students. Ask who to speak to and what they need from you.

  4. Apply when it opens

    Applications open on set dates. Check the dates below and send your application before it closes.

  5. Prepare for the interview

    Practise answering questions about why you chose the route. Ask your school or college for help in building your CVs.

Where are you now?

Years 10 and 11

Choose your T Level

  1. Year 10

    Explore your options

    Find out what T Levels are and how they compare with A levels and apprenticeships. Our quiz suggests which of the five routes might suit you.

    Take the quiz

  2. Year 10 or 11

    Talk to your careers adviser

    They know which schools and colleges near you offer T Levels, and what each one asks for. Take your quiz result with you.

  3. Year 11, usually from the autumn

    Apply to a school or college

    Apply for the T Level course you want. Entry requirements vary, so check them early. Many ask for five GCSEs at grade 4 or above, including English and maths.

    Find a T Level course

  4. Summer, Year 11

    Get your GCSE results

    Your school or college confirms your place. If your results are not what you hoped for, talk to them about your options.

Years 12 and 13

Study and do your placement

  1. September, Year 12

    Start your T Level

    Most of your time is spent learning at your school or college, through practical lessons and projects.

  2. Year 12

    Register your interest

    Tell us which routes you like. We'll email you when Amazon placements open. You can change your answers later.

    Register your interest

  3. Year 12

    Apply through your school or college

    They organise placements for their students. Ask who to speak to, and send your application before the closing date.

  4. Year 12

    Have a short interview

    A conversation about why you chose the route. There are no trick questions. Ask your school or college if they run a workshop where you can practise.

  5. Year 12 or 13

    Do your placement

    Spend at least 315 hours, about 45 days, with an Amazon team. It might be one block, or a day or two each week.

  6. Summer, Year 13

    Finish your T Level

    You get an overall grade: Pass, Merit, Distinction or Distinction*. You need to complete your placement to pass.

Common question

What if I've never worked in a team like this?

That is normal. A placement is for learning. Ask questions and say when you are unsure.

What if I need adjustments?

Tell your school or college and your placement contact what you need before you start. They can plan support, such as changes to your hours, equipment or how tasks are explained.

What if I change my mind about the route?

Talk to your careers advisor as early as you can. You can change the routes you have registered interest in at any time. Even after your chosen T level you can still do over careers that are not related to your course.
